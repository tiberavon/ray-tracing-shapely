# C-3D-01 — A 3D geometry handed to Optiland for a gradient must be differentiable

**Assumptions.** A geometry used in a 3D trace whose result is to be
differentiated supplies a *backward pass*, the derivative of the trace with
respect to the geometry's parameters, not only a forward trace.

**Guarantees.** Given that, a design can be *polished* against a 3D merit
function by gradient methods, not only checked.

**Where it holds.** The plugin's sequential surface, `sms_spline`
(`SMSSplineGeometry`), holds it under Optiland's torch backend, with respect to
the spline's points:
- Optiland's Newton–Raphson intersection gives the first-order derivative
  through the converged hit point (its one-step implicit correction);
- the surface's own inversion of its meridian ends with one attached Newton
  step, so the sag's derivative is the implicit one too.

The gradient of a merit traced through it agrees with a central finite
difference to about 1e-6 relative, and the numpy and torch traces agree to
within 1e-11 on the landing points (6e-12 measured, inside the tolerance at
which Optiland's solve stops).

**Where it does not.** Optiland's non-sequential engine has no plugin hook.
The scene bridge's `SplineRevolutionGeometry` and `AsphereRevolutionGeometry`
are exact but computed in NumPy only: a solid with a designed spline face or
an asphere can be traced and checked in the non-sequential engine, not
polished. A mesh built by revolving sample points is forward-only too.
Supplying one of these to a gradient step silently forfeits the gradient.

**Units and frames.** Not applicable.

**If the assumption is violated.** Deferred: with a forward-only geometry the
failure appears only when an optimisation step is attempted, not when the
geometry is built.

**Checked by.** The plugin's gradient against finite differences, and its two
backends against each other, on the RR lens of Chaves's Example 9.1. Nothing
checks the non-sequential side: there is nothing there yet that provides a
gradient.
